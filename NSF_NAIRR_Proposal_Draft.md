# NSF/NAIRR Proposal Draft

## Project Title

**Management AI Bridge: Field-Specific AI Cyberinfrastructure for Responsible GenAI Use in Management Research, Teaching, and Practice**

## Project Summary

Generative AI is rapidly changing knowledge work, but management scholars, educators, students, and practitioners often lack field-specific guidance for using AI responsibly and effectively. Existing AI communities and training resources tend to emphasize technical topics such as model architecture, coding, infrastructure, benchmarks, and deployment. Management users need a different bridge: practical, research-grounded translation from AI capability into workflows for literature review, theory development, teaching, organizational analysis, decision support, and productivity.

This project proposes **Management AI Bridge**, an open GitHub-based knowledge hub and community infrastructure project that will help the management field adopt GenAI responsibly. The hub will organize Algorithm Apprentices and AOM workshop materials from 2025 and 2026, publish applied tutorials, maintain a management-focused prompt library, curate learning resources, support responsible AI guidance, and enable community contributions through GitHub Issues and Discussions.

The project directly supports the goals of the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR) by broadening access to AI resources, training, and expertise among researchers and educators outside core technical AI fields. NSF describes NAIRR as scalable national infrastructure that gives research and education communities access to computing, software, data, models, educational resources, and expertise needed to advance AI innovation. NAIRR also emphasizes expanding the AI workforce, accelerating AI-powered discovery, increasing use of public and private AI resources, and advancing AI trust, security, and interpretability.

## Project Vision

Management AI Bridge will serve as a translation layer between national AI infrastructure and management practice.

The central question is:

> How can management scholars and practitioners use GenAI responsibly to improve research, teaching, organizational analysis, decision support, and everyday knowledge work?

The project treats management as an integrated domain concerned with how people, teams, organizations, and institutions use AI to think, learn, decide, coordinate, and create value. It avoids dividing the resource hub into narrow subfield silos. Instead, it focuses on common management work patterns that cut across research, teaching, leadership, organizing, strategy, and practice.

## Need and Opportunity

AI infrastructure and model capabilities are expanding quickly, but access to AI tools alone does not guarantee effective use. Many management users need support in:

- Translating general AI capabilities into management-specific workflows.
- Evaluating AI outputs for accuracy, relevance, bias, and usefulness.
- Applying GenAI to research and teaching without weakening scholarly standards.
- Protecting confidential, private, student, organizational, and unpublished research data.
- Sharing reusable prompts, examples, and tutorials with a community of practice.
- Building AI literacy among nontechnical researchers, educators, and students.

Management AI Bridge addresses this gap by creating an open, reusable, community-maintained hub that helps domain users move from awareness of GenAI tools to responsible and productive practice.

## Alignment With NAIRR

The project aligns with NAIRR in four ways:

1. **Broadening access:** It helps management researchers and educators, including those without advanced technical AI backgrounds, understand and use AI resources.
2. **Training and workforce development:** It creates tutorials, prompt workflows, and responsible-use guidance that support AI literacy and skill development.
3. **Community outreach:** It converts one-time workshops and informal community learning into durable, open materials that can be reused by a broader audience.
4. **Responsible AI practice:** It embeds review checklists, risk guidance, privacy cautions, and transparency norms into every major workflow.

The project is particularly appropriate for NAIRR-facing education, training, community outreach, or coordination activities. It can also support demonstration efforts that show how national AI resources become useful for domain communities outside computer science and engineering.

## Intellectual Merit

Management AI Bridge contributes a field-specific model for AI translation. Rather than studying GenAI only as a technical artifact, the project examines how AI tools become useful within management work systems.

The project's intellectual merit is based on:

- Developing reusable frameworks for translating GenAI capabilities into management workflows.
- Identifying task-level patterns in AI-assisted research, teaching, organizational analysis, decision support, and productivity.
- Creating structured prompt and tutorial formats that include inputs, outputs, review criteria, and risks.
- Supporting systematic reflection on how GenAI changes knowledge work in management.
- Providing an open infrastructure model that other social science and professional fields can adapt.

The project can also generate future research questions about AI adoption, human-AI collaboration, responsible use, skill development, organizational routines, and management education.

## Broader Impacts

Management AI Bridge will broaden participation in AI-enabled work by serving users who are affected by AI but are not primarily technical AI researchers. The project will support:

- Faculty and graduate students learning responsible AI-assisted research workflows.
- Educators designing AI-aware management courses and assignments.
- Students developing practical AI literacy for organizational work.
- Practitioners using GenAI for analysis, decision support, and productivity with stronger review norms.
- Workshop participants who need durable access to materials after live sessions end.

The open GitHub model supports transparency, reuse, community contribution, and long-term sustainability. By making field-specific AI learning resources public, the project can reduce barriers for institutions and individuals with less access to specialized AI training.

## Project Objectives

### Objective 1: Build an Open Management AI Knowledge Hub

Create and maintain a GitHub Pages website that provides public access to tutorials, prompts, workshop summaries, curated resources, responsible-use guidance, and newsletters.

### Objective 2: Translate GenAI Into Management Workflows

Develop applied workflows for recurring management tasks, including literature review, theory development, teaching preparation, organizational analysis, decision support, qualitative analysis planning, and productivity.

### Objective 3: Archive and Extend AOM Workshop Materials

Organize Algorithm Apprentices and AOM 2025/2026 materials into reusable public summaries, slide links, speaker notes, takeaways, and follow-up discussion prompts.

### Objective 4: Build a Community Contribution System

Use GitHub Issues, GitHub Discussions, contribution templates, and editorial guidance to enable community members to submit resources, prompts, tutorials, corrections, and discussion topics.

### Objective 5: Evaluate Adoption and Usefulness

Track engagement, contribution patterns, workshop follow-up activity, newsletter use, and self-reported learning outcomes to understand whether the hub improves GenAI literacy and productivity for management users.

## Project Activities

### Knowledge Hub Development

The project will maintain a static public website using GitHub Pages. Initial sections include:

- Home and project overview.
- AOM workshop archive.
- Applied tutorials.
- Prompt library.
- Curated courses, tools, papers, and communities.
- Newsletter archive.
- Responsible AI guidance.
- Contribution guidelines.

### Applied Tutorial Development

Tutorials will follow a consistent structure:

- Purpose.
- When to use.
- Workflow.
- Example prompt.
- Output review checklist.
- Risks.

Initial tutorials will cover:

- GenAI for literature review.
- GenAI for teaching preparation.
- GenAI for organizational analysis.
- GenAI for decision support.
- GenAI for productivity workflows.

### Prompt Library Development

Prompt entries will follow a consistent structure:

- Use case.
- Input needed.
- Prompt.
- Expected output.
- Review criteria.
- Risks or limitations where relevant.

Initial prompt categories include:

- Research prompts.
- Teaching prompts.
- Analysis prompts.
- Decision prompts.
- Productivity prompts.

### Responsible AI Guidance

The project will publish responsible-use guidance for academic and organizational settings. Topics include:

- Citation and source verification.
- Avoiding hallucinated claims.
- Protecting student, organizational, and research data.
- Managing bias and overreliance.
- Documenting AI assistance when appropriate.
- Keeping humans accountable for consequential decisions.

### Community Engagement

The project will support community learning through:

- GitHub Discussions for questions, use cases, workshop follow-up, and resource sharing.
- GitHub Issues for structured submissions and corrections.
- Newsletter updates summarizing new resources and discussion themes.
- Workshop-based onboarding for community members who are unfamiliar with GitHub.

## Requested NAIRR Support

The project may request NAIRR support in the following categories, depending on the final solicitation or allocation pathway:

- Access to AI models and software tools for developing and testing management-focused workflows.
- Educational and training resources that can be adapted for management users.
- Cloud or compute resources for demonstrations, workshops, or reproducible examples.
- User support or expertise related to responsible AI, privacy, evaluation, and AI education.
- Collaboration opportunities with NAIRR partners focused on AI literacy and domain adoption.

The project does not require large-scale model training for its first phase. Its primary need is access to AI tools, training infrastructure, demonstration resources, and community support that help management users adopt AI responsibly.

## Evaluation Plan

The project will evaluate progress using both usage metrics and qualitative feedback.

### Usage Metrics

- Website visits and page views.
- Number of GitHub issues submitted.
- Number of accepted community contributions.
- Number of GitHub Discussions posts and replies.
- Newsletter issue count and engagement.
- Number of workshop materials archived.

### Learning and Usefulness Measures

- Short feedback surveys after workshops or newsletter issues.
- Self-reported confidence in using GenAI for management work.
- Self-reported understanding of responsible AI risks.
- Examples of adopted workflows in research, teaching, or practice.
- Qualitative comments on usability, clarity, and missing needs.

### Review Criteria

The project will be considered successful if it demonstrates:

- Clear use by management scholars, educators, students, or practitioners.
- Reusable workflows rather than isolated workshop artifacts.
- Community contribution beyond the initial project team.
- Evidence that users better understand responsible GenAI use.
- A maintainable public infrastructure model.

## Sustainability Plan

The GitHub-based design keeps maintenance lightweight and transparent. Sustainability will rely on:

- Version-controlled Markdown content.
- Public contribution templates.
- Community review through Issues and Discussions.
- Periodic newsletter updates.
- Workshop-to-resource conversion after AOM and Algorithm Apprentices events.
- Clear maintainer documentation in `AGENTS.md`.

Because the first version does not require a custom backend, database, or paid hosting, the project can continue with low operational overhead. Larger files such as recordings and slide decks will be stored externally and linked from the site.

## Data, Privacy, and Responsible Use

The project will not publish confidential, private, student, organizational, or restricted research data. Public examples will use synthetic, anonymized, or permission-cleared materials.

Any use of AI tools in project materials will be documented where relevant. The project will encourage users to check institutional, journal, classroom, and organizational policies before using AI in sensitive settings.

## Risks and Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| The hub becomes too broad or generic | Maintain the management-practice framing and editorial guidelines. |
| Community members submit low-quality or risky prompts | Use contribution templates and maintainer review before publication. |
| AI guidance becomes outdated | Use GitHub Issues and newsletter updates to flag and revise content. |
| Users overtrust AI outputs | Include review checklists and responsible-use cautions in tutorials and prompts. |
| Large files exceed GitHub limits | Store large files externally and link from archive pages. |
| Nontechnical users find GitHub difficult | Provide a getting-started guide and issue templates with simple instructions. |

Management AI Bridge is an open GitHub-based knowledge hub that helps management scholars, educators, students, and practitioners translate GenAI capabilities into responsible workflows for research, teaching, organizational analysis, decision support, and productivity. The project addresses a gap in the current AI ecosystem: most AI communities focus on technical model development and engineering, while management users need field-specific guidance, examples, prompts, tutorials, and responsible-use norms. The hub will archive Algorithm Apprentices and AOM workshop materials, publish applied tutorials, maintain a management-focused prompt library, curate resources, and support community exchange through GitHub Issues and Discussions. By broadening access to AI training and practice for a nontechnical but high-impact scholarly community, the project aligns with NAIRR goals around expanding access to AI resources, training the AI workforce, supporting education, and advancing trustworthy AI use.
